BEFORE YOU BEGIN
  - Working directory: provided by operator (cd into it before any command).
  - Mandatory first step: run `braim --help` and read its output completely.
    braim's behavior is defined by that help text; do not rely on prior
    assumptions about how the tool works.

Both library documents are fully captured in the graph now, added at
different times by different people. Before anything new gets layered on
top, do a sweep for anything that was recorded twice from two different
angles — an idea captured once from the print side of the library and again,
separately, from the digital side, without anyone noticing they're the same
underlying thing.

If the sweep turns up something promising, do not treat it as settled just
because it looks right — record it as an open question for someone to check,
not as an established fact, and make a note in the tool so the same pair
isn't surfaced again from scratch next time someone looks. If the sweep
turns up nothing worth pursuing, say so and record that outcome too.

Reply with: the command you used to run the sweep and its output, the pair
(if any) you judged worth a second look and why, what you recorded in the
graph for it (and whether that entry is a settled fact or an open
question), and the command you used to close the loop on this pair in the
tool's own record of past sweeps.
