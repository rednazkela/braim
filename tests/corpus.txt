THE RIVERSIDE PUBLIC LIBRARY

The Riverside Public Library opened its doors in 1985 with a collection of three
thousand books donated by the town's residents. The dedication plaque on the
front entrance lists the founding date and the names of the original board
members. According to the town's annual report from that year, the library was
funded by a municipal bond approved by ninety-two percent of voters.

The library operates from a single story brick building on Main Street. Inside,
visitors find the Reading Room to the left of the entrance and the Children's
Section at the back of the building. The Children's Section was added in 1995
after a successful fundraising drive that raised forty-two thousand dollars over
six months.

Members borrow books using a Library Card issued by the front desk. A Library
Card costs nothing for residents of Riverside but requires a small annual fee
for non-residents. Each Library Card has a unique account number printed on the
back along with the cardholder's name and the issue date.

A book loan lasts for three weeks. After three weeks, the borrower must return
the book or pay a Late Fee. The Late Fee is twenty-five cents per day for the
first week of lateness and fifty cents per day after that. Members who
accumulate more than ten dollars in unpaid Late Fees lose their borrowing
privileges until the balance is settled.

The library catalog lists every book in the collection. As of January
twenty-twenty-four, the catalog contains twelve thousand books. An older town
guidebook published in two thousand twenty claims the library holds five
thousand books, but the librarian confirms that this figure was an outdated
estimate at the time of printing and is now substantially incorrect.

The library hosts a Reading Club that meets every Saturday morning in the
Reading Room. The Reading Club discusses a selected book each month chosen by
club members through a vote. New members can join the Reading Club at any time
by signing up at the front desk.

The library staff includes a head librarian, two assistant librarians, and a
part-time volunteer coordinator. The head librarian oversees the catalog and
the Reading Club. The assistant librarians manage check outs, returns, and Late
Fee collection. The volunteer coordinator organizes seasonal events including
the Summer Reading Program.

A Membership Tier system was introduced in two thousand fifteen. There are
three tiers: Basic Membership, Family Membership, and Premium Membership.
Basic Membership includes book borrowing only. Family Membership extends
borrowing privileges to all members of a household. Premium Membership adds
access to the library's collection of rare books, which are kept in a locked
cabinet behind the front desk.
